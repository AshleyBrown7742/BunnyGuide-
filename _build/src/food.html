The Basics

Lean into, take a break from

Eat foods that feed your soil, and take a break from the ones that fill the bucket. This is 80%, not all-or-nothing.

Lean into
  • Leafy greens and cruciferous veggies (broccoli, cabbage, brussels sprouts)
  • Beets, artichoke, garlic, onion, lemon
  • Clean protein: wild fish, pasture-raised eggs, organic poultry
  • Healthy fats: olive oil, avocado, nuts, seeds
  • Fiber-rich foods every day
  • Fermented foods: sauerkraut, kimchi, plain yogurt or kefir
  • Fresh herbs at every meal
  • Filtered water through the day
Take a break from
  • Alcohol
  • Added sugar and refined carbs
  • Fried foods and seed oils
  • Processed and packaged foods
  • Excess caffeine
  • Conventional dairy, if it bothers you
The Bunny Method Tip

An 80% effort you can keep up beats a 100% effort you quit by Wednesday.

Shop Smart

The Dirty Dozen & Clean Fifteen

You don't need to buy everything organic. Just the produce that carries the most pesticide residue. Frozen organic counts and is usually cheaper. Lists are updated yearly by the Environmental Working Group (EWG).

Dirty Dozen: buy organic

Strawberries · Spinach · Kale, collard and mustard greens · Grapes · Peaches · Pears · Nectarines · Apples · Bell and hot peppers · Cherries · Blueberries · Green beans

Clean Fifteen: conventional is fine

Avocados · Sweet corn · Pineapple · Onions · Papaya · Sweet peas (frozen) · Asparagus · Cabbage · Watermelon · Mushrooms · Mango · Sweet potatoes · Kiwi · Honeydew melon · Carrots

Veggie Haters Welcome

Picky eaters

If the taste or texture of vegetables is a dealbreaker, forcing it isn't the answer. There are real ways to close the gap.

Disguise tricks that work

  • Blend spinach or zucchini into marinara, chili or fruit smoothies
  • Stir riced cauliflower or broccoli into regular rice
  • Swap pasta for zucchini or hearts of palm noodles in a sauce you love
  • Grate carrot or zucchini into muffins or banana bread

Balance Your Plate

Alkaline vs. acid-forming foods

Like soil, your body does best with the right pH. Your body controls your blood pH tightly on its own, but your daily food choices shape the load it has to balance. You don't need to eat 100% alkaline. Just tip most meals toward the first column.

CategoryAlkaline-forming (favor)Acid-forming (limit)
GreensSpinach, kale, chard, arugula, collards, romaine, broccoli, cauliflower, brussels sprouts, cabbage
Other vegetablesCucumber, celery, carrots, beets, zucchini, sweet potato, asparagus, peppers, onion, garlic, mushrooms
FruitsLemon and lime, avocado, watermelon, cantaloupe, berries, apples, bananas, pears
Nuts, seeds, legumesAlmonds, flax, chia, lentils, most beansPeanuts
Animal proteinRed meat, processed and deli meat, pork, poultry, shellfish, eggs (mildly)
DairyCheese, milk, conventional yogurt
GrainsWhite bread, white rice, pasta, packaged cereals
SweetenersRaw honey, in moderationRefined sugar, candy, pastries
FatsOlive oil, avocado oilSeed oils (canola, soybean, corn, vegetable)
DrinksHerbal tea, lemon water, filtered waterAlcohol, soda, energy drinks, excess coffee
ExtrasGinger, turmeric, cinnamon, apple cider vinegar, fresh herbsArtificial sweeteners, fried foods
Keep your protein

Eggs and fish are acid-forming but still important for muscle, fullness and repair. Keep them. Fill the rest of your plate with plants.

Check with us first

Start with 12:12 for at least a week before tightening. Black coffee, plain tea and water don't break a fast. If you feel dizzy, shaky or unwell, eat. Ask us first if you take blood sugar medication, are pregnant or breastfeeding, or have a history of disordered eating.
